Harender S. Dhattarwal
Postdoctoral Research Associate · Remsing Lab · Rutgers University

I am a postdoctoral research associate in the group of Prof. Richard C. Remsing at the Department of Chemistry and Chemical Biology, Rutgers University. My research focuses on developing and applying machine learning interatomic potentials, ab initio molecular dynamics, and classical simulation to understand ion transport in solid-state electrolytes and the structure of electrochemical interfaces — with the goal of informing the rational design of next-generation energy storage materials.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemistry at IIT Delhi in 2022 under the supervision of Prof. Rajesh K. Kashyap, where I studied the microscopic structure and thermodynamics of ionic liquids, deep eutectic solvents, and electrolyte solutions using molecular simulation. During my PhD I also collaborated closely with Prof. Jer-Lai Kuo at the Institute of Atomic and Molecular Sciences (IAMS), Academia Sinica, Taiwan, on quantum effects in hydrogen-bonded systems. Prior to IIT Delhi, I earned an M.Sc. in Chemistry from IIT Guwahati (2017) and a B.Sc. in Chemistry (Honours) from Ramjas College, Delhi University (2015).

My research philosophy centers on building physically transparent, computationally efficient models that retain quantum mechanical fidelity where it matters most. I am actively seeking collaborations in computational electrochemistry, ML potential development, and solid-state battery materials, and I am on the academic job market for faculty and research scientist positions beginning in 2026.

Service & Outreach

TCCB Seminar Series — Organizer

I organize the Theoretical and Computational Chemistry and Biology (TCCB) monthly seminar series at Rutgers University — a forum that brings together computational chemists, biophysicists, and materials scientists from across the department and beyond. The series features invited talks from leading researchers in the field, fostering cross-disciplinary collaboration and providing visibility for computational methods in the broader chemistry community. Seminars are held monthly (typically the second Thursday) at 12:00 PM in Wright-Rieman Labs, with Zoom access available.
