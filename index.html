Advisor: Prof. Richard C. Remsing
Google Scholar / GitHub / LinkedIn / Remsing Lab / Email

I am a computational materials chemist working at the intersection of statistical mechanics, quantum chemistry, and physics-informed machine learning. My research aims to understand and predict the behavior of complex condensed-phase materials—including superionic conductors, solid-state battery electrolytes, and electrified electrochemical interfaces—directly from atomic-scale simulations.

Currently, as a postdoctoral researcher in the Remsing Lab at Rutgers University, my work centers on developing machine learning interatomic potentials with rigorous long-range electrostatic formulations, elucidating non-classical ion transport mechanisms (such as the electronic paddle-wheel effect in solid electrolytes), and modeling heterogeneous solid-liquid interfaces under applied potentials.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemistry at IIT Delhi in 2022 with Prof. Hemant K. Kashyap, focusing on the microscopic structure, dynamics, and interfacial capacitance of ionic liquids, deep eutectic solvents, and water-in-salt electrolytes. During my doctoral studies, I was also a visiting researcher collaborating with Prof. Jer-Lai Kuo at the Institute of Atomic and Molecular Sciences (IAMS), Academia Sinica, Taiwan.

Research Focus

01

Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials & Long-Range Polarization

Standard machine learning interatomic potentials typically truncate interactions beyond a short radial cutoff, fundamentally failing to describe long-range electrostatic screening, dielectric breakdown, and polarization response in polar fluids. We build physics-informed architectures that couple equivariant neural network representations with self-consistent electrostatic models. This enables quantum-accurate simulations of dielectric saturation in water and complex electrolyte mixtures across tens of nanometers and microsecond timescales.

DeepMD Dielectric Saturation Equivariant Potentials Long-Range Electrostatics
02

Solid-State Electrolytes & Superionic Conduction

All-solid-state batteries require inorganic solid electrolytes with room-temperature ionic conductivities comparable to liquid electrolytes. Using ab initio molecular dynamics and electronic structure methods, we uncovered the electronic paddle-wheel mechanism in fast-ion conductors such as silver iodide (AgI) and thiophosphates. Fluctuations in local electron density and dynamic cation-anion polarization act cooperatively to lower activation barriers for cation transit, challenging traditional rigid-lattice conduction paradigms.

Solid-State Batteries Superionic Conductors Electronic Paddle-Wheel AIMD
03

Electrochemical Interfaces & Solvation Under Confinement

Energy storage kinetics and stability are governed by the nanostructure of the electrical double layer at polarized electrodes. We study interfacial ordering, desolvation penalties, and solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation in water-in-salt electrolytes (WiSE), ionic liquids, and deep eutectic solvents at electrified carbon and metal surfaces. In confined environments, our simulations demonstrate how confining pore wall flexibility can induce capillary dewetting and govern molecular transport.
