Research

Computational approaches to understand materials from first principles

Theme 01

Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials

Density functional theory delivers near-chemical accuracy for small systems but remains computationally prohibitive at the length and time scales required to sample thermodynamic properties of complex materials. Neural network potentials (NNPs) trained on DFT data bridge this gap — retaining quantum mechanical accuracy while enabling nanosecond-scale molecular dynamics of thousands of atoms.

My work extends standard short-range NNPs by incorporating physically motivated long-range electrostatics. These models reproduce the dielectric properties of polar liquids — including dielectric saturation of water under strong electric fields — even beyond their training domain. This extrapolation capability arises from the physically grounded architecture, not memorization of training configurations.

Current applications span water, concentrated electrolyte solutions, and electrochemical interfaces where long-range polarization dominates solvation structure and ion dynamics. These potentials provide the quantum-accurate molecular foundation for understanding capacitance, ion transport, and electrolyte behavior across many orders of magnitude in concentration.

Theme 02

Solid-State Electrolytes & Superionic Conductors

Superionic conductors — materials that transport ions as efficiently as liquids while remaining mechanically solid — are the foundation of next-generation solid-state batteries. Understanding the microscopic origins of order-disorder transitions that produce superionic phases is a central open problem in solid-state chemistry.

In collaboration with the Remsing group, I discovered that the electronic polarizability of anion sublattices plays a previously unrecognized role in enabling fast ion conduction. In AgI and Na₃PS₄-type compounds, thermally activated fluctuations in electron density create dynamical paddle-wheel motions of anion electron clouds — transiently opening low-barrier pathways for cation hopping that would not exist in a classical picture.

This insight reshapes design criteria for superionic materials: optimizing electronic polarizability of the anion framework, not just structural geometry, is a direct handle on engineering ionic conductivity. Ongoing work examines hidden quantum disorder — spatially disordered but locally correlated electronic fluctuations — as a universal feature of superionic phases.

Theme 03

Electrochemical Interfaces & Electrolyte Design

The performance of electrochemical devices — from batteries to supercapacitors to carbon-capture electrolyzers — is fundamentally governed by structure and dynamics at electrode-electrolyte interfaces. These buried interfaces are notoriously difficult to probe experimentally, making molecular simulation an indispensable complement to experimental electrochemistry.

I apply AIMD and ML-MD to characterize the electrical double-layer structure, ion solvation shells, and adsorption behavior at graphite and transition-metal electrodes in water-in-salt (WiSE) electrolytes, ionic liquids, and fluorinated ether systems. Special focus is on how confinement, concentration, and applied potential reshape interfacial ion distributions and modify transport coefficients relative to bulk.

Emerging directions include constant-potential simulations using the Siepmann–Sprik model and grand canonical ML-MD, enabling direct comparison with electrochemical impedance and cyclic voltammetry. These approaches are essential for understanding SEI formation chemistry, electrostatic capacitance, and molecular-scale design of electrolytes for carbon capture in concentrated KOH and amine systems.
