Research Program

Theoretical & Computational Materials Chemistry

Developing physics-informed machine learning potentials, uncovering unconventional ion-transport pathways in solid-state electrolytes, and designing electrified electrochemical interfaces from first principles.

Theme 01

Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials & Long-Range Interactions

Accurately capturing polarization, dielectric response, and non-local electrostatic screening across extended length and time scales.

The Challenge: Standard local machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) rely on local cutoff spheres (typically 4–6 Å) and fail to capture non-local dielectric breakdown, electronic polarization, and collective charge fluctuations that dominate polar liquids and battery electrolytes.
Our Approach: Integrating long-range physical electrostatic models (e.g. self-consistent polarizable charges, Ewald summation) with deep neural network representations (DeepMD-kit, equivariant tensor networks).
Key Discovery: Quantified dielectric saturation of liquid water under external electric fields up to the megavolt-per-centimeter regime with quantum mechanical accuracy, resolving long-standing discrepancies between continuum Booth theory and empirical force fields.
Representative Publications
JPCB 2023 Dielectric Saturation in Water from a Long Range Machine Learning Model (DOI: 10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c00390) JCC 2021 First-principles Investigation of Polyolefin Photo-oxidation (DOI: 10.1002/jcc.26702)
Theme 02

Solid-State Electrolytes & Superionic Conduction

Discovering novel mechanisms of ultrafast cation transport in inorganic solid electrolytes for all-solid-state batteries.

The Challenge: Solid-state lithium and sodium batteries promise dramatic improvements in safety and energy density, but ion conductivities in crystals are typically hindered by rigid lattice barriers and high hopping activation energies.
The Electronic Paddle-Wheel: Identified an electronic analog of the classic molecular rotor paddle-wheel mechanism. In superionic conductors such as silver iodide (AgI) and thiophosphates, dynamic electronic polarization and localized cation clustering work cooperatively to open low-energy transit channels.
Broader Impact: Established physical design rules to engineer lattice polarizability and cation disorder in halide and chalcogenide fast-ion conductors.
Representative Publications
Nat. Commun. 2024 Electronic Paddle-Wheels in a Solid-State Electrolyte (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-44274-z) ChemPhysChem 2025 Electronic Paddlewheels Impact the Dynamics of Superionic Conduction in AgI (DOI: 10.1002/cphc.202500077) JCP 2025 Electronic Fluctuations, Cation Clustering, and Ionic Dynamics in Molten Silver Iodide (DOI: 10.1063/5.0301431)
Theme 03

Electrochemical Interfaces & Confined Systems

Nanoscale solvation, interfacial capacitance, and phase transitions in water-in-salt electrolytes and ionic liquids.

The Challenge: Superconcentrated water-in-salt electrolytes (WiSE) and room-temperature ionic liquids expand battery voltage windows to over 3.0 V, but the molecular origin of electric double-layer capacitance and Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) stability remains hotly contested.
Our Findings: Revealed how hybrid ionic liquid co-solvents break through the 21 m concentration limit of standard WiSE, suppressing parasitic water splitting at electrified graphite surfaces. Modeled how nanoscale confining material flexibility promotes capillary dewetting and accelerates ion transport.
Redox-Active Nanopores: Demonstrated with experimental collaborators how redox-active multifunctional metal nodes in coordination cage nanopores can modulate local Brønsted acidity by up to 12 pKa units, providing unprecedented control over proton-coupled electron transfer.
